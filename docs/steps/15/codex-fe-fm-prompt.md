## The user's complaint (2026-11-12, after looking at the map from F2 to F4)

"It is the shittiest map I ever saw. It is so blurry. And the UI around it is hard to use." They compare it with OneSoil's field-selection map, which is sharp and smooth. This is the product's core screen: it MUST look sharp at field level and be easy.

Capture screenshots over Tadla (32.40 N, 6.55 W), Beni Mellal (32.37 N, 6.34 W) and the Gharb (34.3 N, 6.3 W) at zoom 10, 12, 14, 15 and 16, and save them under docs/evidence/live/2026-11-12-fm/before/. Record in the notes, with numbers, every cause of blur and every usability problem you can observe: imagery native resolution (NASA HLS is 30 m, the MODIS base is 250 m at tile level 9, many HLS days are empty tiles), tile source maxzoom and tileSize, raster-resampling, device pixel ratio, canvas size versus CSS size, parent tiles shown while children load, raster fade, overzoom, the date stepper picking dates with cloud or no image, and anything else.
2. Also record every usability problem on the map screen and the shell around it at the three widths: for example the large banners that fill the top of the screen when a service check fails, the stuck 'Chargement de la carte...', console errors and 404s, the empty list panel, tiny hit targets, the Add field menu, unreadable controls, the layer popover, the legend, keyboard operation, and the dev-server warnings 'Matched leaf route at location /map does not have an element'.

## Step 2. Make the imagery sharp (the main fix)

NASA HLS and MODIS are the right data to ANALYSE and to show by date, but they are too coarse to be the default picture of a field. Find and choose, with real side-by-side screenshots at zoom 14 to 16 over Tadla and Beni Mellal, the best FREE, key-less (or free-key you can record without secrets) high-resolution base imagery for Morocco, and record the decision, licence and exact attribution in docs/DECISIONS.md and THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md. Candidates to evaluate: EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless mosaics (10 m, WMTS, check the licence terms for non-commercial open-source use and the newest year available), the NASA GIBS layers (HLS 30 m, Landsat, MODIS), Esri World Imagery (verify its terms for this use before choosing), OpenAerialMap and any other free source you can verify. Never choose a source whose terms forbid this use, and do not use Google, Bing or OneSoil tiles. Implement the winner as the DEFAULT base layer from world zoom to zoom 17 with the sharpest native resolution available and smooth overzoom, and keep NASA HLS (by date, with the date stepper and compare slider) and MODIS as selectable 'NASA image' layers over it, with the existing real-date probing. The first paint of a field must show sharp imagery, never a blurry placeholder; while tiles load show the previous sharp level, not an upscaled coarse one.
Also fix the rendering settings that cause blur whatever the source: tileSize and @2x handling for retina, `raster-resampling`, `maxzoom` per source, no CSS scaling of the canvas, `pixelRatio`, `fadeDuration` and `raster-fade-duration`, `maxTileCacheSize`, and avoid two stacked maps unless the compare slider is on.

## Step 3. Orientation labels (free, no self-hosting)

Add roads, towns and place labels over the imagery from a free key-less vector tile source with an acceptable licence (for example OpenFreeMap or Protomaps public builds; verify terms and attribution), with French and Arabic names where present, readable over imagery (halo), lazy-loaded, and clearly attributed. If no acceptable source exists, say so and keep imagery only. Do not build or host tiles.

## Step 4. Make the map screen easy (use the OneSoil screenshots as the benchmark)

Fix everything found in step 1 and at least: (a) failures of backend checks never fill the top of the screen with large banners: use one compact, dismissible, non-blocking status chip with a 'Retry' link in the shell header, with the detail in a popover; (b) the map renders immediately with a skeleton the size of the final map, never a stuck 'loading' text, and works with WebGL checks and a clear fallback message when WebGL is unavailable; (c) route and console cleanliness: no 404s on load, no React Router warnings, no unhandled errors; (d) controls: zoom in and out, locate, layers, each at least 40 px with clear icons, visible focus, keyboard operable; the Add field button is a clear primary action; the field list panel has a proper empty state and loads fields; (e) smooth zoom: wheel and pinch ease continuously (about a quarter level per wheel notch, damped), double-click and buttons animate over 300 ms, `flyTo` for locate and for selecting a field; (f) the date stepper and layer popover are simple: pick 'Satellite photo' (sharp base) or 'NASA image' with a date list, with a one-line plain explanation 'NASA images are 30 m and may be cloudy'; (g) the legend and attribution are tidy and always visible but small; (h) at 390 px the map fills the screen with a bottom sheet for the field list and a tab bar, no overlapping controls; at 768 and 1440 the three-panel system matches OneSoil's density and spacing (measured tokens: 14 px base, two weights 400 and 500, 8 px control radius, 20 px pills, dark rounded chips on the map, compact 32 px controls); (i) performance: 60 fps pan and zoom, first sharp paint under 2 s on the dev machine, map chunk lazy-loaded, no needless re-renders (React profiler check in the notes).
